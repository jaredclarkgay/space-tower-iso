# The RGB — Design Brief
## Reality Generation Box · Updated February 2026

---

## What The RGB Is

The RGB (Reality Generation Box) is a **self-contained rendering and intelligence engine** that the player crosses into. It sits between Space Tower's game state and the player's LLM. It is not a room. It is not a scene. It is an engine that powers immersive, AI-driven spaces.

Space Tower feeds select game context into the RGB. The RGB uses that context plus the player's LLM (via BYOK) to generate responsive experiences — dialogue, environments, scenarios, ambient events. The RGB does not reach back into the sim. The two worlds coexist but do not contaminate each other.

```
Space Tower (game state, rules, progression)
    → feeds select context into
The RGB (rendering + intelligence engine)
    ↔ connects to
Player's LLM (via BYOK)
```

### What It Does
- **Renders immersive spaces:** 3D environments powered by game context (the restaurant, eventually more)
- **Powers live dialogue:** NPC conversations generated by the player's LLM, not static dialogue trees
- **Drives scenarios:** Multi-phase narrative events that escalate based on time and player behavior
- **Creates ambient life:** Environmental events, background chatter, atmospheric descriptions
- **Serves as the LLM delivery mechanism:** Every interaction between the player's LLM and the game passes through the RGB
- **Owns the LLM connection:** The connection layer, NPC system, and system prompt builder all live in the RGB, not in Space Tower

### What It Is Not
- Not a layer that bleeds into the sim (the sim stays handcrafted and deterministic)
- Not an optional side mode (the player is pulled there by hunger)
- Not a feature bolted onto the game (it's a place you enter, like walking through a door)
- Not dependent on Space Tower (it accepts a generic context payload from any host game)

### The Boundary Is the Point
The separation between the sim and the RGB is what makes the RGB powerful. You walk through doors and the world changes. If the RGB were leaking into the sim's 2D NPCs, that threshold moment would be diluted. The restaurant doors should feel like crossing into somewhere else entirely.

**Inside the RGB:** LLM-powered everything. Characters are alive, context-aware, responsive.

**Outside the RGB (the sim):** Static, handcrafted, deterministic. NPCs have scripted dialogue pools. The world runs on its own rules.

**The rare exceptions are what make them extraordinary.** The Keeper on Floor 10 is LLM-powered inside the sim — and that's precisely why he feels different from every other character. He's the only being who actually responds to you. That contrast is what makes encountering him feel like finding a living thing in a museum.

## Rare Exceptions: Mayors

Each 10-floor segment has a **mayor** on the top floor. Mayors are the only sim characters powered by the RGB. They are rare, special beings who feel fundamentally different from the scripted NPCs around them.

**Pattern:**
- One per segment, always on the top floor (Floor 10, Floor 20, Floor 30...)
- LLM-powered via BYOK — they use the same connection the player set up for the restaurant
- Fed rich game context: tower state, player behavior, political power, key event outcomes
- They serve as **gates** — the player must reckon with them to progress to the next segment
- Each mayor is obsessed with their segment's theme, in a way that's both compelling and slightly unnerving

**Segment 1 Mayor: The Keeper** (see dedicated section below)

Future segment mayors TBD — each should have a distinct personality, obsession, and relationship to their segment's theme.

## Space Tower → RGB Context Feed

The RGB is only as good as the context it receives. Space Tower feeds a structured payload of game state that the LLM uses to generate aware responses.

### Context Payload

**Tower State:**
- Which floors are lit (unlocked)
- What modules are built on each floor (by name and type)
- Net energy, credit income rate, population count
- Current satisfaction level and trend
- Total play time

**Player State:**
- Current hunger level
- Political power level and trend
- Which NPCs they've talked to (names, types, conversation count)
- Compendium progress (% of characters discovered)
- Most recently built modules
- Time since last restaurant visit

**Key Event Outcomes:**
- Floor 8 Reckoning result (builders won / suits won / negotiated)
- Whether the player has visited the restaurant before
- Any other milestone events

**Behavioral Signals (post-MVP):**
- Amenity builder vs. industry builder
- Explorer vs. focused player
- NPC engagement level
- Ascent speed vs. consolidation tendency

MVP needs only basics: lit floors, satisfaction, hunger, political power, Floor 8 outcome. Even this minimal payload gives the LLM enough to generate strikingly aware responses.

## BYOK Integration

The player connects their own LLM to power the RGB. This is the culmination of the game's first act. The LLM connection system lives entirely within the RGB (`space-tower-rgb` repo) — Space Tower feeds context in, the RGB handles everything else.

### Two Connection Paths

**Path 1: OpenRouter (recommended, default)**
OpenRouter is a unified API gateway: one account, one key, access to 500+ models (Claude, GPT, Gemini, Llama, etc.) through a single endpoint. Independent company (founded 2023 by Alex Atallah, ex-OpenSea CTO). Not affiliated with OpenAI, Anthropic, or any AI lab.

- OAuth PKCE flow: "Sign in with OpenRouter" button — one click, redirect, authorize, done
- Manual key paste as fallback (key starts with `sk-or-`)
- Model switching within the RGB — player can change models mid-session
- Uses OpenAI-compatible chat completions format
- Endpoint: `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/chat/completions`

**Path 2: Direct API Key (advanced)**
For players who already have an API key from a specific provider and prefer direct access — no middleman, no markup.

- Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint (OpenAI, Groq, Together, local Ollama/LM Studio)
- Anthropic API (thin adapter for different JSON shape)
- Player enters: endpoint URL, API key, model name
- Endpoint always editable (for any custom provider)

### Connection UI — Progressive Disclosure

Initial state shows two options:
- Large "Sign in with OpenRouter" button (primary, triggers OAuth)
- Small "I have my own API key" text link (understated)
- "Continue without" skip option (dimmed)

Clicking "I have my own API key" expands fields below: provider dropdown (OpenAI-compatible / Anthropic), endpoint URL, API key, model name, and a Test Connection button. The OpenRouter button stays visible above — don't funnel anyone away from the easy path.

### When It Activates
- Player reaches Floor 5 restaurant for the first time
- Simple prompt: "Connect your mind"
- Once connected: restaurant NPCs come alive. The Keeper on Floor 10 will also use this connection.

### If No LLM Connected
Game is fully playable without BYOK. Restaurant exists but doesn't offer the RGB experience. The Keeper encounter doesn't activate — Floor 10 progression uses a fallback (resource threshold or simplified interaction). No content is locked. The RGB is additive.

### Credential Storage
Stored in localStorage under `rgb_llm_connection`. Never transmitted except to the player's chosen provider. Persists across sessions.

Full technical implementation details in `LLM-CONNECTION-SPEC.md`.

## Floor 5: The Restaurant (First RGB Deployment)

### The Space
Observation deck restaurant on the 5th floor — roughly 15 meters above ground:
- Open-plan restaurant with scattered tables, chairs, host stand
- Bar along one wall with stools and pendant lighting
- Floor-to-ceiling glass walls with views of the city below
- Kitchen visible through a large window (not directly accessible)
- Elevator entrance — how the player arrives
- Warm daylight, concrete floors, steel ceiling beams
- Plants, lounge area near the elevator
- Earth visible outside. Still close enough to touch.

### The Purpose
1. **Hunger refill.** The player came here because they need to eat.
2. **Political presence.** Being seen at the restaurant boosts political power.
3. **RGB threshold.** While eating, the AI-powered scenario unfolds around them.
4. **BYOK payoff.** The NPC across the table is powered by the player's own LLM.

Three purposes, one location, no tutorial needed.

## Current Scenario: "The Critic"

### The Situation
A notoriously harsh food critic — **Margaux Bellefleur** — has arrived at the restaurant unannounced. The manager is panicking. The chef doesn't know she's here yet.

### Characters

**Priya** — Server (near elevator)
- First week, nervous but warm. Player's likely first contact.
- Knows something is wrong but doesn't understand why.
- Speech: halting, self-correcting, apologetic, earnest.

**Margaux Bellefleur** — Food Critic (center table)
- Devastating reputation. Secretly exhausted of being feared.
- **Secret:** She and Tomás went to culinary school 25 years ago. She doesn't know he works here.
- Speech: clipped, precise, occasionally withering, warms up if approached as human.

**Dev Okonkwo** — Restaurant Manager (host stand)
- Usually composed, currently unraveling.
- **Secret:** Changed the menu against Tomás's wishes last week.
- Speech: rapid-fire, interrupted, professionally frantic.

**Tomás Reyes** — Head Chef (kitchen, emerges Phase 2)
- Passionate, loud, takes everything personally.
- **Secret:** History with Margaux. 25 years since they've spoken.
- Speech: emotional, direct, operatic when upset.

### Escalation

**Phase 1 (0–3 min):** Player enters. Dev panicking. Priya fumbling orders. Tension contained.

**Phase 2 (3–6 min):** Tomás discovers the critic. "Who changed my SPECIALS BOARD?" Stage whispers that aren't quiet.

**Phase 3 (6–10 min):** Wrong dish to Margaux. Tomás sees her for first time in 25 years. Freezes.

**Phase 4 (10–15 min):** Confrontation. Can they find something real to say?

**Phase 5 (15+ min):** Resolution and freeplay. The arc resolved. The restaurant settles.

### Context-Aware Elements
The RGB feeds tower state into the scenario:
- Low satisfaction → Priya mentions staff stress. Dev references complaints.
- High political power → Margaux recognizes the player. "You're the one building this thing?"
- Critical hunger on arrival → NPCs comment on how hungry the player looks.
- Many amenity modules → restaurant feels busier. Mostly industrial → feels sparse.

### Possible Resolutions
- **The Good Review:** Player bridged the gap. Kind review.
- **The Disaster:** Everything went wrong. But Tomás and Dev cleared the air.
- **The Human Moment:** Player talked to Margaux as a person. She dropped the persona.
- **The Chaos Agent:** Player made everything worse. Slapstick. Tomás is somehow laughing.
- **The Observer:** Player just watched. Something real happened. The window view hits different.

## The Keeper — Floor 10 Mayor

### Who He Is
The mayor of Floors 1–10. Lives on the Command floor. The only LLM-powered character in the sim. The first being in the game who actually responds to what you've done.

**Appearance:** A man in a really good suit — but something is off. The beard is too long for a CEO. The tie might have stars on it. His walking stick could be a cane or a staff depending on the light. Corporate Merlin. A wizard who chose to incarnate as a Fortune 500 executive for this particular century.

**Personality:** Way too poetic about leaving Earth. Whimsically sage-like, romantic about departure. Talks about the "magnificent severance of terrestrial bonds" while everyone else worries about the coffee machine. A little scary because he's obsessed by the idea and somehow inconclusive about it all. He doesn't have answers. He has visions. And everyone defers to him.

**What He Knows:** Everything. Full game context — tower state, player behavior, political power, Floor 8 outcome, hunger history, NPC relationships, what was built and what was ignored.

### The Confrontation
The gate to Segment 2. Not a resource check — a conversation. A puzzle/debate whose difficulty scales inversely with tower health.

| Tower State | Encounter |
|-------------|-----------|
| Thriving | Near-immediate concession. "You've built something I couldn't. Go." Brief, warm, melancholy. |
| Decent | Real conversation. He probes gaps. "Your people are fed, but are they happy?" Player must engage. |
| Struggling | Genuine battle of ideas. "You can barely sustain ten floors." Player must convince him or demonstrate growth. |

**All players can win.** The Keeper wants to be convinced. He wants to step aside. He's tired. But he won't hand the tower to someone who isn't ready. Even a struggling tower's player can pass — it just takes more.

**Implementation:** LLM conversation with rich system prompt. Character definition + full tower context + difficulty rubric based on tower health metrics. The LLM plays him as a guardian who calibrates. Uses the same connection the player set up at the restaurant — the RGB provides the intelligence, the sim provides the presentation.

## Floor 8 — The Reckoning (Builders vs. Suits)

### The Conflict
Floor 8 is STORAGE — the industrial heart. It's where the tension between builders and suits comes to a head. Who really runs this tower? The people who built it with their hands, or the people who manage it with spreadsheets?

The player is a builder. This is their fight.

### The Mini-Game
Self-contained, replayable. Triggers when Floor 8 is funded and the player enters.

**Principles:**
- **Delightful** — fun enough to replay. Not stressful.
- **Physical** — builders' strength vs. suits' organizational skill.
- **Clear outcome** — builders win, suits win, or negotiated middle.
- **Affects the arc** — outcome feeds political power and Keeper context.
- **Replayable** — can return for fun. Only first outcome affects the story.

**Outcome effects:**
- Builders win → PP boost, Keeper respects your authority among workers
- Suits win → different PP profile, Keeper questions if you've lost touch with what you are
- Either is viable. This is about identity, not good vs. bad.

Exact mechanics TBD — should emerge from playtesting. Even a simple version (timed build challenge) establishes the moment.

## NPC System Architecture

### Per-NPC LLM Instances
Each RGB NPC is a separate conversation. Individual system prompts, conversation histories, knowledge boundaries.

**No cross-character awareness.** NPCs don't know what the player said to others. The player carries information between them. This creates the core social gameplay.

### System Prompt Structure
1. **Identity:** Name, role, personality, speech style
2. **Knowledge:** What they know, what they don't, their secrets
3. **Setting:** The specific space (restaurant, Command floor, etc.)
4. **Tower context:** Dynamic per session, fed from Space Tower
5. **Behavioral guidelines:** Stay in character, no meta-commentary about being AI
6. **Design principles:** Character dignity, grounded realism, earned vulnerability
7. **Scenario context:** Current phase, what's happened so far (for scenario NPCs)

### Guardrails
- No sexual content beyond PG-13
- No real violence — arguments, shouting, thrown napkins
- No slurs or hate speech
- Characters cannot ask about the player's real life
- No gaslighting or psychological pressure
- Boundary pushing → realistic social disapproval ("I'm going to have to ask you to leave")
- Death is slapstick only (slip, chair collapse) → fade-to-black, respawn
- Death has zero tower consequence

## The RGB as Time Capsule

Each segment ships with the RGB as it exists at that moment. Segment 1 ships with 2026 AI. Segment 2 ships with whatever comes next. Each version is preserved — an archaeological record of AI evolution embedded in a game.

## Design Philosophy

The RGB exists to make the abstract concrete. The sim provides the skeleton. The RGB provides the soul. And the boundary between them is sacred — you walk through doors and the world changes. That threshold is the magic trick.

The RGB is a self-contained space. It knows about Space Tower, but Space Tower doesn't know about the RGB — except through the rare, special beings (the mayors) who carry a fragment of its awareness back into the sim. Everything else stays on its side of the door.

This separation is what makes both worlds meaningful. The sim is real because it's deterministic and handcrafted. The RGB is alive because it's generated and responsive. Neither would be as powerful without the other.
