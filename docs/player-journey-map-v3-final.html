Dialogue Rule

Suits always call it "their work." Sterilized. Abstract. Important-sounding. They never say "building" or "construction" — it's "the work," "our work," "the project."

Builders always call it "their job." Concrete. Physical. Matter-of-fact. "The job," "my job," "this job."

This difference is subtle but load-bearing. It shows up in every speech bubble, every NPC line, every piece of dialogue in the game.

Two Guidance Systems

Big yellow arrows — label critical objects. "BUILD" on the seesaw. "ENTER" on the door. Always visible. Mechanical.

Suit speech bubbles — social guidance. Ambient, rotating, contextual. 3–5 seconds visible, then fade. Never more than 2–3 on screen. Pool of lines per game phase.

Foundation Sync

The tower foundation must be visually synced between exterior (3D) and interior (2D). Not a huge grey block — a series of wide, stepped tiers (like long steps) leading up to the entrance door. Same design language in both views. The interior ground floor reflects the stepped foundation. 1:1 wherever possible.

The Arrival — Exterior Only

The player doesn't enter the tower until they've built enough floors to be curious. The exterior IS the game at first. ~10 suits surround you — they're wearing hard hats for this kickoff. They hired you. They brought the seesaw you asked for. They are waiting. This group persists on the exterior permanently (distinct from the commuter suits who come and go in cars).

01 Spawn surrounded by hard-hat suits Suits
~10 suits in hard hats, gathered near the seesaw, facing you, idle-animated. Speech bubbles appear with enough time to read (4–6 seconds each). Each suit has a few unique lines. They rotate.
"There you are. We've been waiting." "The seesaw's here — just like you spec'd it." "Construction crew's on the roof already." "The board fast-tracked the budget. Don't ask how." "Everything's approved. Just need you to start the work." "We've got steel, concrete, and... coffee?"
These suits are the permanent exterior crew. They don't despawn. They're distinct from commuter suits (who arrive/leave in cars). They wear hard hats at the kickoff — later they may take them off. Each one has 3–4 unique speech bubble lines that rotate with enough display time to read fully.
02 Yellow arrow: BUILD → Seesaw tutorial moment Exterior
Big floating yellow arrow on the seesaw: "BUILD." Also: a small interactive ? icon at the base of the launcher — press it for a brief explanation of what to do.
First-time animation (3.4 seconds): On the very first seesaw use, a brief cinematic plays: camera pulls wide to an elevation view, demonstrates the action — you jump off the pedestal, hit the seesaw, crate launches skyward, *POOF* character teleports back onto the platform, a new crate appears on the seesaw. This only plays once. After that, gameplay is immediate.

The ? button is always available at the base of the launcher for anyone who needs a reminder. Small, unobtrusive, press-to-read.
03 First launch → Suits CHEER Exterior
Crate hits the bullseye. The suits erupt:
"YES!" "Direct hit!" "That's why we hired you." "One down."
On miss: "Close one." "Wind, probably." No punishment, just social feedback.
04 Build Floors 1–4 on exterior Exterior
Keep launching. 2 crates per floor. Tower grows. Suits comment on progress. Between launches, explore — climb the tower, try the crane. Bulldozer is NOT visible yet.
"Two floors already. The work is on schedule." "Lobby's taking shape." "At this rate we'll be orbital by Tuesday." "The crane's yours too, if you want it."
Suit commentary shifts as floors rise. Early: excitement. Mid: impressed. By floor 3-4: some drift toward the tower entrance, hinting there's something inside. They don't tell you to go in — they migrate.

05 Suits hint at the interior → Yellow arrow: ENTER Suits
After 3–4 floors, speech bubbles shift. Some suits walk to the entrance. Second yellow arrow appears on the door: "ENTER."
"Have you seen inside yet?" "The lobby's ready for inspection." "People are starting to move in." "You should see what they did with your quarters."

Interior Discovery Begins

The loop establishes: build outside, explore inside. The suits got you building, got you curious, pointed you at the door. From here they become background characters on the exterior — still commenting, but no longer the primary guide. The interior has its own language: NPCs arriving, floors lighting up, the platforming itself.

06 Enter tower → Discover Floors 1–2 Interior
Walk through the stepped foundation, through the front door. Floors are lit — you built them outside. 3 stages each: Power On → Structure → Activate. F1 Lobby Reception, first worker. F2 Quarters Corner store + diner flanks. Bunks.
The foundation tiers are visible from inside too — the ground floor reflects the stepped exterior design. The diner flank is NOT yet activatable (needs water + crop first). Gene appears on Floor 1 — forgettable. "Do you know how many forms it takes to approve a floor? Seventeen."
07 Floor 3 — The Garden of Eden Eden Moment
F3 Garden The floor has tomatoes and fruit ripening on the planters. You MUST eat the first fruit to proceed to Floor 4. The moment you eat: the hunger gauge appears in the HUD for the first time. 🧑 [██████████] — full, green. Your character has become finite. Hunger will now decay forever.
The Garden of Eden moment. Before this, the player is invincible — no weakness, no needs, no decay. Eating the fruit is the departure. Your character shows vulnerability for the first time. The hunger bar materializes. From now on, you need to eat.

Progression gate: Cannot advance past Floor 3 until fruit is eaten. This is the one mandatory interaction in the interior.

Visible water pipes: Floor 3 has visible pipe infrastructure — translucent pipes with flowing water visible through/beneath the planter boxes. Activating water (stage 2) is what makes the first crop ready immediately. The pipe is visible, physical, real.
▼ hunger bar appears — you are now mortal
08 Diner unlocks → Floor 4 exploration Interior
Now that water is on and a crop is ready, the Floor 2 diner flank activates. You can eat there too. F4 Research Labs, data streams. Gene on Floor 3 — still forgettable.
Diner activation chain: 1. Turn on water (Garden Floor 3, stage 2) → 2. First crop is ready (immediate after water) → 3. Diner flank on Floor 2 becomes active. This is a visible, physical dependency — not a hidden threshold.
09 Food chain → Bulldozer unlocks (+ Control Room) Unlock
Food chain complete + happiness threshold → bulldozer appears outside (not visible before this). Control Room (basement) also becomes available at this moment. "🚜 BULLDOZER UNLOCKED — exit the tower to find it."
The bulldozer is not visible or hinted at before this unlock. It materializes. The Control Room bulldozer view also becomes available simultaneously. On the exterior, suits react: "They cleared you for the dozer?" "Try not to hit my car."

FUTURE: Bulldozer upgrades via achievements. Weekly competitive mini-games.
10 Drive the bulldozer Exterior
Pure fun. Deform terrain. Suits scatter if you drive toward them. No progression gate — a reward for reaching this point.

Floors 5–8 · Tension grows · Factions form · The Reckoning
11 Build Floors 5–8 — crate count escalates Exterior
Crate count increases each floor now. The builders get sloppier the higher they go — wasteful, dropping things. You send more because that's just how it is. Suits notice:
"They dropped another one?" "The work requires more materials at this altitude." "It's a job, not a warehouse."
The escalating crate count is both a difficulty curve and a narrative beat: the higher you build, the more chaotic the construction. Builders aren't malicious — they're just sloppy at altitude. It's honest. It's how building works.
12 Floor 5 — The Restaurant + THE ARGUMENT Interior
F5 Restaurant Activate the floor. Eat here to refill hunger. Camera zooms 30% closer on this floor — encouraging the player to look closely. The door hums (RGB threshold, future). Gene is here. Still forgettable.

THE BREAKING POINT: A suit and a builder are arguing about window views. Who gets the office with the view? They follow you, speech-bubbling at each other. ~2 minutes of looping dialogue. Funny but real.
"Our work requires natural light. It's in the productivity studies." "Your work? I installed that window. With my hands." "Nobody's disputing that. We're discussing allocation." "Allocation. That's a word for stealing." "It's a word for organizing. Which is literally our job— our work." "You organize paper. I organize steel."
This argument cannot be resolved. It loops. The player can't fix it. It's the tension that will define the rest of the game. The argument is funny — both sides have legitimate points. Neither is the villain. But the crack is visible now.

After this floor, suit speech bubbles across the game start to shift. They're still supportive, but they begin expressing a thirst for space. They need more offices. It's not personal — it's simply necessary for "the work" to expand. (What IS their work? They don't say. They always sterilize it. Secretly, their work IS tower building — but they'd never call it that.)
13 Floors 6–7 — Factions crystallize Interior
F6 Lounge Music, books. F7 Observation Telescopes. Cruise through these. NPCs are hilarious and faction-y — they express the nature of each floor AND annoyance with the other side.
"Have you been to seven? They're measuring the telescope for curtains." "The observation floor needs a scheduling system. You can't just... look." "This tower's getting too tall for its own good." "Growing pains. Every project of this scale has them."
Characters should be positive about their own side AND critical of the other — but not too heavy-handed. Both sides are likeable. Both have valid complaints. One builder or suit should directly express: "This tower's getting tall and unwieldy." Others echo the feeling in their own words. The faction tension is the air everyone breathes now.
14 Floor 8 activates → THE RECKONING Interior
F8 Storage Stage 3 + all floors lit = The Reckoning begins. No opt-out.
▼ the tower shakes
15 THE RECKONING — Builders vs. Suits Set Piece
Blackout. Briefing. The same suits who cheered your launches now fight you for floors. 90 seconds: claim blocks by standing. 12 builders vs. 18 suits. Gene is absent.
The dramatic irony is complete. They welcomed you. They funded you. They cheered. Now they want what you built. The window argument on Floor 5 was the crack — this is the earthquake.

Post-reckoning: choose your builder color. Rematch bell. Gene returns: "Not seen since the Reckoning." Flood of 30 civilians — population explosion.

Post-Reckoning Shift

Permanent tone change. Exterior suits are quieter. Subdued bubbles. Some are conspicuously absent. The cheerleading is over. The power dynamic has shifted. You proved something on Floor 8.
